## Summary

**The 15% target is met on this window**: the champions' error is 46.6% lower than seasonal naive's (WAPE 16.8% against 31.5%) on the held-out window, the last 13 complete weeks (w/c 2026-07-06 to 2026-09-28), pooled over the 11 SKUs that have a seasonal-naive forecast and over horizons 1-26. **That window is off-season**: it holds no festive peak and misses the summer peak, so it measures off-season performance, not the festival timing the models were designed for. The festive-season breakdown below comes from the selection window (Oct-Nov 2025, the window the champions were picked on, so it flatters them), and the forward oracle test scores the production forecast across Diwali 2026 against expected demand.

Pooled WAPE is volume-weighted: the bearing (about 5,000 units a week) dominates it and the Gift Box (about 20) barely registers. Per SKU, the median skill is 33.6%; the champion does not beat seasonal naive on COL-CFN-1200; it beats it by less than 15% on IND-MTR-1HP. The oracle, which knows the true expected demand, scores 1.5%: what is left between it and the champions is still reducible. The champions' 80% intervals cover 76.1% of held-out weeks (target 75-85%); **not a fully clean held-out number**: the interval method was changed because of the first held-out result (see Intervals).

## What worked and what didn't

- **Picking a champion per SKU did worse than using Prophet everywhere** on the held-out window (WAPE 16.8% against 14.3%). The per-SKU pick was made on the selection window, where several SKUs' winners were decided by small margins; on fresh weeks some of them lost. Not changed after seeing this: re-picking on the held-out window would make its headline meaningless. A pick that needs a clear margin, or one model for all, is the thing to test next, on the selection window.
- ETS: held-out WAPE 19.3%, 38.6% lower than seasonal naive; selection window 19.4%.
- Prophet: held-out WAPE 14.3%, 54.6% lower than seasonal naive; selection window 18.2%.
- LightGBM: held-out WAPE 17.5%, 44.5% lower than seasonal naive; selection window 13.1%.
- Seasonal naive is biased low (-19.6% held-out): it repeats last year and misses growth. It also cannot move a festival: Diwali was 11 days earlier in 2025 than in 2024.
- **A supply shock in the last training week drags the next forecasts down.** WAPE at horizons 1-4 when the week before the origin was a shock week (80 forecast-weeks per model), against every other origin: ETS 17.0% against 20.3%; LightGBM 34.4% against 15.4%; Prophet 26.7% against 18.5%; Seasonal naive 25.8% against 30.1%. Shocks are not forecastable and are never an input, but a model should not carry one forward: ETS on the log scale moves least, LightGBM's last-weeks lags and Prophet's trend carry it. Example: the bearing's week of 2026-08-31 sold 667 units against about 8,000; at the next origin LightGBM forecast 2,825 for a week that sold 8,226. Not fixed here (it was found on the held-out window); a shock-robust level, such as a 13-week median instead of a mean, is the change to test on the selection window.
- **LightGBM's 80% intervals miss the 75-85% band** where it is champion (71.5%); the Air Fryer pulls it down (40.0%): a new product's errors are wider than the long-history SKUs' errors its pooled intervals come from. Reported, not recalibrated on the held-out window.
- **FDS-NMK-200 misses the Diwali 2026 peak**: its champion (Prophet) peaks in the week of 2026-10-12; expected demand peaks in the week of 2026-11-02.
- **KIT-IND-2000 misses the Diwali 2026 peak**: its champion (ETS) peaks in the week of 2026-12-14; expected demand peaks in the week of 2026-11-02 (ETS has no calendar: it repeats last year's shape).
- Champions per SKU: COL-ACL-70 Prophet, COL-CFN-1200 LightGBM, COL-TFN-400 LightGBM, FDS-GFB-1KG Prophet, FDS-NMK-200 Prophet, FDS-SWT-500 Prophet, IND-BRG-6205 LightGBM, IND-MTR-1HP LightGBM, IND-PMP-05 Prophet, KIT-AFR-45 LightGBM, KIT-IND-2000 ETS, KIT-MXG-750 LightGBM.
- Short history, outside the headline: KIT-AFR-45 (launched Feb 2026, no seasonal-naive forecast exists before Feb 2027). LightGBM only, its yearly pattern borrowed from its category: held-out WAPE 29.6%, 80% coverage 40.0%.

## The oracle floor

The oracle forecasts the ground truth's expected demand, so its error is pure Poisson sampling noise: the floor no model can beat. 'Reducible' is how much of seasonal naive's excess error over the floor the champions remove: (naive - champion) / (naive - oracle).

## Intervals

Every model's 80% and 95% intervals come from its own backtest errors, log((actual + 1) / (forecast + 1)), as empirical quantiles per model and horizon group (1-4, 5-13, 14-26), pooled across SKUs. The held-out window's intervals use selection-window errors only; production uses every backtest error.

**Recalibrated once, and the held-out result is what triggered it.** The first version used quantiles per SKU, and its held-out coverage came out low (champions 67.5% at 80%, backtest run 1). That result is why the method was revisited. The replacement was then chosen on a split inside the selection window (calibrated on targets before 2026-04-06, measured on the rest), where the same failure showed: a SKU's champion is picked because its selection errors were small, so those errors understate what comes next. Choosing on the selection window does not undo the trigger: **the held-out coverage of the pooled method is not a fully clean held-out number**, because the held-out window had already been seen when the method changed. Both versions:

| Method | Champions' 80%, selection split | 95%, selection split | Champions' 80%, held-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per SKU (first version) | 63.4% | 81.3% | 67.5% (backtest run 1) |
| Pooled per model (used) | 82.5% | 91.8% | 76.1% (measured after the change) |
