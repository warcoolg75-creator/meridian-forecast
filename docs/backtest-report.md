## Read this first

- **The held-out window has now been looked at twice**, once for v1 and once for v2. Every v2 change was decided on the selection window, on a split inside it (chosen on targets before 2026-04-06, judged on the weeks from then to the held-out start), and the held-out window and the forward oracle test were then re-scored once, with nothing changed afterwards. But what prompted the changes came from v1's held-out results, so **v2's held-out figures are a second look, not a clean out-of-sample result**.
- **The festival changes were motivated by v1's Diwali-check failure** (4 of 6 festive champions peaked in the Diwali weeks of the forward oracle test). In v1 the Namkeen's Prophet forecast peaked in w/c 2026-10-12, three weeks before expected demand's peak (w/c 2026-11-02), and the Induction Cooktop's champion was ETS, which has no calendar. That result is why Prophet's festival regressors and LightGBM's festival features were revisited. The replacements were chosen on the selection window, by a rule written before looking at their effect on Diwali 2026 (merge festivals that are within a week of each other in every year; no parameter tuned on the two festive seasons), but the trigger was a forward-test result, so **v2's Diwali check is not a clean test either**.
- **The interval caveat from v1 still holds**: the interval method was recalibrated because of v1's first held-out result (see Intervals), so held-out coverage is not a fully clean number in either version. v2's wider intervals for short-history SKUs were prompted by v1's held-out Air Fryer coverage (40% at 80%) and decided on a simulation inside the selection window.
- A bug was found while building v2: LightGBM's "mean level" setting silently used the median for every SKU after the first (a parameter shadowed by a loop variable). It affected only the v2 experiments' copy of the v1 LightGBM (13.6% selection WAPE instead of v1's own 13.1%), never v1's runs and never the adopted median setting. Fixed and covered by a test; the LightGBM decision below is stated against v1's real 13.1%.

### Prophet: merged festivals and a dip rule

**Festival merges.** The rule: festivals among Prophet's regressors whose dates fall within 7 days of each other in every year of the calendar become one combined regressor (their window columns summed). Weekly data cannot tell such festivals apart, so separate regressors get large opposite effects that cancel in the past but not when the two dates fall differently against the weeks. Merges this run made: dhanteras + diwali; durga_puja + dussehra. Dhanteras and Diwali, always two days apart, were checked for the same problem: they have it, and are merged too. No prior was tuned; the default regressor prior is kept.

**The dip rule.** A week whose units collapsed is left out of Prophet's fit, so a supply shock in the last training weeks does not bend the trend. Selection-window experiments:

| Prophet | Selection WAPE, headline SKUs | Festive SKUs |
|---|---|---|
| v1: no merges, every week fitted | 18.2% | 16.0% |
| Festival merges only | 17.8% | 16.5% |
| Merges + trailing dip rule | 13.6% | 14.9% |
| Merges + dip rule against trailing and year-ago weeks | 17.4% | 16.0% |
| Merges + the same, trailing alone in the first year (adopted) | 14.0% | 14.8% |

This run's Prophet selection WAPE: 14.0% (the experiments' adopted row: 14.0%).

## Intervals

Every model's 80% and 95% intervals come from its own backtest errors, log((actual + 1) / (forecast + 1)), as empirical quantiles per model and horizon group (1-4, 5-13, 14-26), pooled across SKUs; since v2, a SKU with under 52 weeks of data gets the young-SKU table instead. The held-out window's intervals use selection-window errors only; production uses every backtest error.

**Recalibrated once in v1, and the held-out result is what triggered it.** The first version used quantiles per SKU, and its held-out coverage came out low (champions 67.5% at 80%, backtest run 1). That result is why the method was revisited. The replacement was then chosen on a split inside the selection window (calibrated on targets before 2026-04-06, measured on the rest), where the same failure showed: a SKU's champion is picked because its selection errors were small, so those errors understate what comes next. Choosing on the selection window does not undo the trigger: **the held-out coverage of the pooled method is not a fully clean held-out number**, because the held-out window had already been seen when the method changed. Both versions (selection split recomputed this run):

| Method | Champions' 80%, selection split | 95%, selection split | Champions' 80%, held-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per SKU (first version) | 72.6% | 80.3% | 67.5% (backtest run 1) |
| Pooled per model (used) | 83.3% | 90.0% | 79.7% (measured after the change; v1 76.1%) |

## Bias

ETS, Prophet and LightGBM forecast log(1 + units) and transform back, which gives a median, not a mean. That suits WAPE but reads low on skewed SKUs. Not corrected; reported:

|---|---|---|---|
| Seasonal naive | -19.6% | -14.8% | -16.0% |
| ETS | -6.5% | -4.0% | -4.3% |
| Prophet | +5.4% | +4.6% | +3.1% |
| LightGBM | -4.0% | -2.0% | -7.1% |
| Champions | +0.7% | +1.3% | -2.0% |

Negative = forecasts too low. Per-SKU bias is in the champion table above.
